# R-062 · Component parentage conflates ownership with many-to-many composition

## Context

P1 correctly separates Domain scope nesting from the Service/Module component structure and from
scope participation. The registry represents component structure with one nullable
`component_parent` per Service or Module. The docsite publishes this parent edge with kind
`composes`. Required/provided contract blocks separately produce directed `requires` edges when an
interaction has a shared wire schema.

Real component models can contain both an ownership/decomposition hierarchy and reuse: one Module
may be owned in one place while several Services call it, or a Service may orchestrate several
components that retain independent ownership. Domain participation also needs the "stated role"
required by P1.

## Expected

The Protocol gives each relationship one unambiguous meaning and cardinality. A reader can tell
whether a parent owns a component, contains its implementation, merely uses its interface or
coordinates it. Reusable components do not need duplicate identities to appear in every consumer's
composition. A component's Domain participation identifies not only the Domain ID but its local role
in that scope.

Behavioral relationships without a shared data schema still have a named direction and meaning, as
required by P1.

## Observed

`SpecTarget.component_parent` is a single string or null, and cycle validation treats it as a
hierarchy. Documentation and the publication graph call the resulting relation `composes`, a term
that commonly describes usage and can be many-to-many. The registry has no distinct ownership field
or general directed relationship collection.

`participates_in` is only an array of Domain IDs. It cannot carry the role that P1 requires. The
embedded contract mechanism represents a subset of interactions with schemas but does not represent
all calls, constraints, ownership assignments or coordination relationships.

## Impact

Architects can encode the same real relationship in incompatible ways: choose one arbitrary
component parent, omit other consumers, misuse Domain participation, or rely on prose that the graph
and structural validator cannot distinguish. The hierarchy may appear to claim exclusive
composition even when the component is shared. Domain contexts also cannot obtain a stable role from
the bare participation edge.

This is a model-semantics ambiguity rather than proof that one specific replacement schema is
required. The ownership, containment, use and contract concepts need explicit decisions before the
field becomes a durable Profile 8 contract.

## Evidence

- `protocol/principles.md` P1 names component composition, scope participation with a stated role,
  and directed behavioral relationships as separate concepts.
- `src/concorde/specification/repository.py#SpecTarget` defines one `component_parent` and a string-only
  `participates_in` tuple.
- `src/concorde/specification/repository.py:179-193` validates component parentage as a single acyclic
  chain and participation only as known Domain IDs.
- `docsite/plugins/scoped-content/model.ts` publishes `component_parent` as a `composes` edge and bare
  participation as `participates_in`.
- `.concorde/specs.json` assigns each Module exactly one Service parent even where the Module
  participates in several problem scopes.
